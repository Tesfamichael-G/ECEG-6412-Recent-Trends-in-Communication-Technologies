    ECEG-6412 — Recent Trends in Communication Technologies¶

    MSc in Communication Engineering · Year 1, Semester I · Elective I

    This course surveys the wireless propagation channel, modulation and multiple-access techniques, and the major generations of mobile systems: from AMPS/TACS through GSM and UMTS to LTE, WiMAX, 5G, and the emerging application of machine learning to physical- and MAC-layer problems.

    What you will learn¶

    • Understand the wireless channel

      Path loss, shadowing, multipath, Doppler, and the fading models (Rayleigh, Rician, Nakagami) that shape every wireless link.

    • Apply modulation, coding, and multiple access

      BPSK/QPSK/QAM/OFDM, TDMA/FDMA/CDMA/SDMA, error-control and source coding — the toolbox behind every cellular standard.

    • Analyze 2G–5G cellular systems

      AMPS/TACS → GSM → UMTS → LTE → 5G NR: architecture, physical layer, protocols, and evolution.

    • Explore emerging trends

      Wired technology trends, NGN (Bluetooth, ZigBee, WiFi, UWB), IoT, and the emerging role of machine learning in the PHY and MAC layers.

    Chapters¶

    • Introduction

      History of wireless, standards landscape, layered cellular view, wireless and cellular environments, propagation channel, legacy AMPS/TACS.

      Chapter 1

    • Modulation and Coding for Wireless Systems

      Analogue and digital modulation (BPSK, QPSK, QAM, OFDM), multiple access (TDMA, FDMA, CDMA, SDMA), error control and source coding.

      Chapter 2

    • Wired Technology Trends

      Access loop, core network, and distribution networks — the wired backbone that mobile systems depend on.

      Wired Trends

    • GSM

      System architecture, physical layer, channels, data and services, 2.5G evolution (HSCSD, GPRS, EDGE, EGPRS).

      Chapter 3

    • UMTS

      Migration from GSM, architecture, physical layer, coding, network design, and 3.5G/3.75G (HSDPA, HSUPA, HSPA).

      Chapter 4

    • Advanced Broadband Wireless

      4G technologies, LTE, WiMAX, network architecture, capacity, radio components.

      Chapter 5

    • Next Generation Networks

      NGN overview, PANs (Bluetooth, ZigBee), WiFi, UWB, 5G and IoT.

      Chapter 6

    • Machine Learning for Communications

      The emerging-technology initiative: ML for the PHY and MAC layers across wireless, optical, satellite, and molecular communication.

      Chapter 7
