| Component | Weight | Timing |
|---|:---:|---|
| Tests (3) | **30%** | Weeks 4, 11, 14 |
| Assignments (4) | **20%** | Weeks 3, 7, 10, 15 |

---

## Tests (30%)

Three 45-minute in-class tests, weighted **10% each**. Closed book; one handwritten
A4 formula sheet allowed; non-programmable calculator permitted.

| Test | Covers | Learning outcomes |
|:---:|---|---|
| **Test 1** (week 4) | Ch 1 — Introduction, channel, legacy | LO1, LO2, LO5 |
| **Test 2** (week 11) | Ch 2 (multiple access), Ch 3 (GSM), Ch 4 (UMTS) | LO4, LO6, LO7 |
| **Test 3** (week 14) | Ch 5 (4G/LTE/WiMAX), Ch 6 (NGN) | LO8, LO9 |

Note: **LO3** (modulation) and **LO10** (ML for comms) are assessed in assignments
and the final exam, not in the mid-term tests.

## Assignments (20%)

Four take-home assignments at **5% each**, roughly one per part:

| Assignment | Released | Due | Focus |
|:---:|---|---|---|
| **A1** | Week 1 | Week 3 | Link budget, channel modelling, fading |
| **A2** | Week 6 | Week 7 | Modulation, multiple access, coding |
| **A3** | Week 9 | Week 10 | GSM / UMTS comparison, network planning |
| **A4** | Week 13 | Week 15 | 4G/WiMAX/NGN analysis; ML for comms case study |

Each assignment is a mix of analytical problems (theory + numeric) and a short
research/analysis component. Expected length: 6–10 pages including figures.
Typewritten PDF; code in appendix if used.

**Rubric per assignment:**

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|:---:|
| Correctness of final answers | 40% |
| Rigour of derivations | 20% |
| Interpretation and discussion | 25% |
| Presentation and references | 15% |

## Final examination (50%)

3 h, closed book, one handwritten A4 formula sheet. Covers the entire syllabus,
weighted roughly proportional to teaching hours:

| Part | Weight in final |
|---|:---:|
| I — Basics (Ch 1–2) | 40% |
| II — Wired trends | 5% |
| III — Wireless networks (Ch 3–4) | 30% |
| IV — Emerging technologies (Ch 5–7) | 25% |

**Structure.** 5–7 multi-part questions spanning theory (definitions, derivations)
and applied analysis (link budget, capacity, comparison). Diagrams are permitted
and encouraged where relevant.

---

## Late submissions

10% per calendar day, capped at 3 days. After 3 days, submission scores zero.
Extensions only for documented medical or family emergencies; request before
the deadline.

---

## Academic integrity

All submitted work must be your own. Collaboration on understanding is encouraged;
collaboration on submitted answers is plagiarism. Plagiarism results in a zero on
the affected component and a report to the Dean's office. See the
[Feedback](feedback.md) page for reporting errors in course material.
